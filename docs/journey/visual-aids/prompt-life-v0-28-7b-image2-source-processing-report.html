HTML Caption and Callout Copy

CardCaptionCallouts
Costs We Must Count AI answers depend on infrastructure, energy and water, labor, privacy, governance, and institutional choices. Infrastructure: Data centers, chips, energy, water, and e-waste are part of the real system.
Human systems: Labor, privacy, bias, and information quality depend on deployment choices.
Institutions: Governance, incentives, and access shape who benefits and who bears costs.
Risk vs Myth Model-literate users separate practical risks from unsupported myths, then choose review and safeguards. Practical risks: Privacy exposure, hallucination, tool misuse, bias, and overreliance need concrete safeguards.
Unsupported myths: Conscious chatbots, hidden omniscience, and secret self-training claims need evidence.
Review: Risk literacy asks what system, context, tool, and institution are actually involved.
Model Literate Synthesis Model literacy connects mechanics, evidence checking, and human responsibility without treating fluency as wisdom. Mechanics: Prompts become tokens, vectors, states, scores, probabilities, and generated tokens.
Evidence: RAG, grounding, citations, and sources help only when the support actually matches.
Responsibility: People remain accountable for use, review, governance, and decisions.
The Perfect Storm Data, compute, storage, methods, labor, and incentives converged into modern LLM capability. Many inputs: Data, compute, storage, and methods each mattered.
Human systems: Labor, funding, products, and demand helped move capability into use.
Convergence: Modern LLMs came from conditions meeting, not one single breakthrough.
Human-Centered AI AI output should support human judgment; people remain accountable for decisions, review, and governance. Support: AI output can help people draft, notice, compare, or review.
Judgment: High-stakes decisions still need human responsibility and local context.
Accountability: Review, appeal, policy, and governance belong to human institutions.
